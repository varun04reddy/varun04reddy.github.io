Humans have evolved and grown in terms of intelligence. We think abstractly and communicate complex ideas through language and mathematics. It seems like we have fully evolved from our primal beings into something more symbolic.
That being said, something that amazes me every day is our self-awareness. We have the capacity to ponder existential thoughts such as: What is the fundamental nature of reality? Why are we here? What happens after we die? These questions have been pestering man for centuries.

Recently, it seems like society is facing this turning point. Our brains, while they are remarkable, are undoubtedly finite. When pondering some of life’s biggest questions, we have rationalized our lack of understanding as something natural, maybe some things just are not meant to be figured out. This turning point is led by the emergence of artificial intelligence, which has rapidly proven both breadth and depth in humanity’s most complex fields and there seems to be no sign of slowing down. At this pace, what can this intelligence not do? What is my purpose in the world now? Overall, what is humanity’s edge? Its unsettling to think that years of human effort could be trumped by a web of matrix multiplications. AI’s feats have naturally led all of us to reflect on our role in society, career, and self-worth.

However, I would argue that humans still have a great edge, something that might be more exciting and allow us to be more human. Here I will present my argument, along with a brief ideas for a paradigm of training AI models that will facilitate humanity to maximize their inherent strengths.

I argue that the AI of the future will be humanity’s next companion. Rather than replacing what we value, it will add a new dimension to our relationships and our own understanding of ourselves. Artificial Intelligence will be key to maximizing man’s self-conscious nature: the urge to explore, ask questions, and think deeply about ourselves and the world around us. This would require an intelligence that can constantly process multiple streams of inputs just like humans, continuously learning, and capable of knowing when to listen, when to ask the right questions, and when to keep digging deeper. It would evolve alongside us, resonating with our emotions and mirroring our complexities. My current idea of this would be a system that experiences life with you, understands the nuances of your experiences, and uses that understanding to provide meaningful interventions (this comes with massive privacy and cognitive implications, things that I often think about and might write another piece on).

Creating this system will essentially have to recreate important aspects of the human brain and how humans function. Specifically, what differentiates it from traditional chatbots is the continual learning. Rather than just in-context learning and updating, this continual learning should have a hybrid of in-context learning while also updating weights if the information is deemed non-temporary and important. I am confident that the space of AI, neuroscience, and cognitive science will converge drastically if we show or prove that this proposed training structure or strategy is viable and can scale. Secondly, I believe that reinforcement learning is very important and intriguing. When applied to closed-loop scenarios, it has shown remarkable and unexpected results. I hope to think that it can be applied to open scenarios if we develop an intermediate or [spontaneous](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05611-2) reward generator, something similar to internal feedback mechanisms humans inherently have.

I can imagine a scientist using such a companion as an extension of his mind, bouncing off hypotheses and accelerating discovery. A writer leaning on it to sift through entangled thoughts. A student could find in it a teacher that adapts to how they think. A philosopher could walk along a beach, speaking to the companion about the nature of time, guided by questions nudging them into deeper thought. And perhaps a confused teenager, unsure of their place in society and what they want to do with their life, could ask it questions they are too embarrassed to ask anyone else.

Then again, we are definitely not at a point where AI can truly serve as this kind of companion, and we have identified areas where it still lacks fundamental qualities. Still, when I see the pace of progress, I cannot help but imagine and hope that this future is closer than it seems. I picture a time where humans, supported by these companions, are free to accelerate discovery and uncover deeper truths about themselves and the world around them.
